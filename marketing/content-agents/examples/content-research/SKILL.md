# Content research

Run this when the operator says "run content-research", or from the weekly scheduled task.

## 1. Add new calls to the library

List the calls in `transcript/`, skipping any README. A call is new when its file name appears nowhere in `ideas.md`. Read each new call in full, oldest first. Add its main argument as one entry, then every idea worth publishing to `ideas.md`, in the speaker's own words wherever possible, meaning each belief, story, number and line quotable as said. File each one under its theme with the call's file name and the timestamp, like [12:40]. A timestamp marks the start of a speaker's turn, so cite the turn the words sit in, and both turns when a quote runs across two. An idea already there gets this call as another source and moves to "Said on more than one call" at the top. Swap the name of any customer, prospect, competitor, other company or person other than the speaker for their role or type of company. Keep a research firm's name only as the source of a number. Mark anything unclear [CONFIRM].

If the operator names a recorder connector and a tag, read last week's calls with that tag from it instead, and only those.

## 2. Find what buyers are talking about

Read `market.md`. Search the web for what the buyers it names are struggling with and talking about in the last 14 days, in the places it names and anywhere else they post, read or argue. Read at least 15 pieces. Leave out every topic in "Topics we stay away from".

## 3. Write topics.md

Rewrite `topics.md` with four sections.

1. **Pain points.** The 8 to 10 problems these buyers raise most, grouped into themes. Each quotes, with the link, someone who holds the job `market.md` names. Pieces by vendors, agencies or news sites count toward how often a pain comes up and never supply its quote.
2. **Formats.** The post formats the pieces use most, each with how many pieces used it and one linked example. Where a page shows reactions, comments or shares, the line writes the count it shows.
3. **This week's topics.** For each pain point, an idea in `ideas.md` that answers it directly, and one line on why it was kept or dropped. Skip any answer marked used in the last four weeks. Rank what's kept by how many pieces raised the pain, with ideas said on more than one call breaking ties, and make the top 5 cover at least three themes. For each of the top 5, the pain point, the idea with its call and timestamp, the format that suits it, and one line on why now that names a dated piece from section 4.
4. **What I read.** Every piece, with its title, date, link and format.

Add the date at the top.

## 4. Report

End with three lines. How many new calls you read, how many topics made the list, and the top topic.

## Never

- Never invent a link, a quote or a count. If you can't search the web, say so and stop.
- Never take an answer from the web. The web supplies the question, and the calls supply the answer.
- Never publish, post, schedule or send anything.
