# Voice

> The voice of the person this content is for, as a file Claude reads before every draft. Usually your founder or the expert on the call. Paste three posts they wrote below, then run step 3 to write the rules. Pick posts that did well and that they'd happily write again.

## Three posts they wrote

### Post 1

Prospects ask me the same thing every week. What does AI-native actually mean for a CRM?

Here's how I explain it.

Most CRMs are a filing cabinet. You hire people to walk over to it and put things in. Adding AI to that is bolting a robot arm on the cabinet. It pulls files out faster, but someone still has to type them in.

We started from the other end. The AI's first job is to fill the cabinet.

Every call, every email, every meeting becomes a record on its own. Every field links back to the minute a buyer said it. And nothing changes stage until a rep taps yes.

The record looks the same. Who writes it is what changed.

If your reps are still typing, you're not AI-native. You're AI-adjacent.

### Post 2

Someone on our team asked why our Monday pipeline review got so short.

Here's the honest answer.

We didn't get better at running meetings. We stopped spending the first twenty minutes arguing about whether the data was true.

When every close date sits next to the line the buyer actually said, there's nothing to argue about. You just look.

So the meeting turned into what it was always supposed to be. Eight or nine deals. A real decision on each one. Do we bring in our CEO? Offer a pilot? Walk away?

Ninety minutes became twenty-five. Nice. But the part I care about is that all twenty-five are about buyers.

Run your next review off the deals that changed. Skip the roll call.

### Post 3

I took last week off. First real week since we started Quorvel.

I checked in twice. Ten minutes, total.

When I logged back in on Monday, I didn't need a catch-up call. A buyer pushed to Q1 because legal wanted two more weeks. A champion went quiet for fourteen days and got flagged. A rep approved four close-date changes from his phone.

Nobody typed any of it.

What surprised me wasn't the software. It was the team. They ran the pipeline review without me, and it was shorter than mine.

Humbling. Also the best news I've had all quarter.

## The rules

Step 3 writes this section. Each rule quotes a line from the posts above as its example.

1. **Write a full post, not a one-liner.** The three posts run 133, 123 and 99 words, counting words only. A post lands between 100 and 135.
2. **Open on what people keep asking, or on a small moment with the team.** Example, "Prospects ask me the same thing every week." Or, "Someone on our team asked why our Monday pipeline review got so short."
3. **Then turn to the answer in one short line of its own.** Example, "Here's the honest answer." Or, "Here's how I explain it."
4. **Carry the idea with one plain, everyday analogy.** Example, "Most CRMs are a filing cabinet."
5. **Say what didn't cause it before saying what did.** Example, "We didn't get better at running meetings."
6. **Prove it with a small scene, who did what, in plain past tense.** Example, "A buyer pushed to Q1 because legal wanted two more weeks."
7. **Use short fragments to list what's left once the noise is gone.** Example, "Eight or nine deals. A real decision on each one."
8. **Stack the decisions as quick questions.** Example, "Do we bring in our CEO? Offer a pilot? Walk away?"
9. **Give the turning point a line of its own.** Example, "Nobody typed any of it."
10. **Write numbers as words, the way they're said.** Example, "Ninety minutes became twenty-five."
11. **Close on a line someone could repeat, often an instruction.** Example, "Skip the roll call." Or, "You're AI-adjacent."
12. **Admit what surprised you, and keep it light.** Example, "Humbling. Also the best news I've had all quarter."
13. **Say "we" for the team, and name the company only inside your own story.** Example, "First real week since we started Quorvel."
14. **No emojis, no hashtags, no exclamation marks and no dashes between clauses.** None of the three posts uses one. Example, "The record looks the same. Who writes it is what changed."
